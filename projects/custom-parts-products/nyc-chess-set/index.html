Custom 3D Printed NYC-Themed Chess Set

Designing and manufacturing a highly customized resin 3D-printed chess set for an NYC construction firm.

In April 2024, I took on an urgent project for a civil engineer friend. An NYC construction firm needed awards for outstanding architects and engineers to be presented at their end-of-year celebration. The initial idea was to create a trophy featuring a skyscraper, but coordinating each winner’s favorite building proved difficult. Instead, we pivoted to a unique idea: an NYC-themed chess kit. After acquiring the CAD files and STLs for iconic NYC buildings to serve as the main pieces, we needed something distinct for the pawn. It needed to be compact and unique. Since the awards ceremony was taking place near Hudson Yards, we chose the Vessel.

Moving into the engineering phase, I modeled the Vessel in Autodesk Fusion. It took four iterations to achieve a design fully compatible with resin 3D printing, ensuring the complex geometry could be produced reliably. All STL files were carefully edited and scaled to maintain their imposing aesthetic while optimizing the build volume and minimizing the need for supports.

The Vessel modeled in Autodesk Fusion, optimized for resin 3D printing.

To simplify manufacturing, reduce costs, and ensure a rapid turnaround without compromising the premium feel, we decided to print using two different colored resins instead of painting the models. The low-contrast finish minimized the perception of defects while preserving the intricate relief details. We printed all pieces with 100% infill for a solid, high-quality weight.

We ordered 15 magnetic chess boards, discarded the original plastic pieces, and sourced N52 neodymium magnets to ensure our custom pieces would stay firmly in place, even if the board was flipped upside down.

Testing the strong magnetic adhesion of the resin pieces on the board.

The post-processing phase was executed in batches of 15-16 pieces of the same geometry. Each batch first went through a 99% isopropyl alcohol (IPA) bath in dedicated color vats to remove bulk resin residue. Next, they underwent a 3-minute aggressive wash in an ultrasonic cleaner filled with acetone to dissolve any remaining uncured resin. Finally, the pieces were cured in a UV chamber on a low-intensity setting for 2-3 minutes.

By the following week, a fully functional prototype was ready. It was sent to the civil engineer and the firm’s senior architect for approval. The senior architect was so impressed that he kept the sample for himself, ensuring he had one even if he didn’t win an award.

The completed NYC-Themed Chess Set prototype.

For the production phase, the cardboard packaging and resin piece inserts were outsourced, while all the chess pieces were manufactured in-house at my personal workshop. In total, over 600 pieces were printed. Each kit included an extra Vessel and Statue of Liberty (the most complex and delicate geometries) in each color to ensure accidental handling breakages wouldn’t render a kit incomplete.

The entire order was delivered on time. The celebration was a massive success—we received photos from the event showing several chess sets open on dinner tables with attendees actively playing throughout the night.
